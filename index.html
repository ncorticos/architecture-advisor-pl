Where are you building, and in what climate?

This application applies no limit from a regulation. It works from the position of the building and from the climate that position sits in, classified by Köppen–Geiger, because that is the classification the design literature is written against — and, in Poland, placed in the winter zones of PN-EN 12831 and the summer zones of PN-76/B-03420. Name the place, give its coordinates and altitude, then enter the monthly normals of the nearest station. What follows is orientation, compactness, roof, glazing, shading, the organisation of the plan and the movement of air — the decisions taken before any limit can be applied to them.

Monthly normals

Köppen–Geiger is computed, not looked up, so it needs the twelve monthly mean temperatures and the twelve monthly precipitation totals. This application ships none: normals are measured data and it has no authority to invent them. Take them from the IMGW-PIB climate normals for the nearest synoptic station, from another national service, or from a gridded product such as WorldClim, and name the source so the export carries it. The boxes fill themselves in this order: the embedded IMGW station within 5 km of the coordinates above; where there is none, a blend of the nearest stations, weighted by inverse distance; and, on request, web normals — reanalysis for these exact coordinates. Blend nearest and Fetch web normals open only where no station lies within 5 km. Change the coordinates and the choice is made again; type in the boxes and they stay yours.

Written into every CSV and PNG this application exports. A station can still be picked from the list — norms tables at klimat.imgw.pl — or your own source named. Stations, blends and web fetches name themselves.

Grounded in the design literature

Olgyay, Lechner, DeKay and Brown, Hyde, Ching and Shapiro, Brophy and Lewis. Every guideline names the book it comes from, and where the books disagree the application says so rather than averaging them into a single number.

The decisions that come before the limits

Which way the building faces, how compact it is, what its roof does, how much glass each façade carries, where the rooms sit against the sun, and how air moves through the plan. None of these is settled by a maximum thermal transmittance.

A companion, not a replacement

Construction Advisor answers to a regulation and reports what an envelope may not exceed. This one carries only climate zones, as a reading of the territory, and none of any regulation's limits. Use them in that order: shape the building here, then take it there to be checked.

Nothing is uploaded; your work stays in this browser. What leaves it, only to fill something in: the coordinates, to two elevation services when you press Look up and to Open-Meteo when you press Fetch web normals; the map area, to Esri and OpenStreetMap for the tiles; the plot’s position, to OpenStreetMap’s Overpass servers for the neighbouring buildings and, once you move the plot, to Open-Meteo for the ground’s height and to Nominatim for its municipality; an address you search for, to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim.

Classification

Köppen–Geiger, in the formalisation of Peel, Finlayson and McMahon (2007), computed from the normals you enter; for a site in Poland, the winter zones of PN-EN 12831-1 and the summer zones of PN-76/B-03420, from the nearest synoptic station.

Privacy

Nothing is uploaded or stored outside this browser. Coordinates go only to the services that fill something in: elevation (Look up), Open-Meteo (Fetch web normals), the map tiles (Esri, OpenStreetMap), the neighbouring buildings (Overpass), the address search (Nominatim) and, when the plot moves, its height (Open-Meteo) and place (Nominatim).
